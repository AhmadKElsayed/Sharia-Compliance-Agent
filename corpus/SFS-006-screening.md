# SFS-006 — Sector and Financial Screening for Permissible Investment

## §1 Scope

§1.1 This document addresses the screening of companies and assets for inclusion in
Sharia-compliant investment products, funds, and indices.

§1.2 Screening has two stages: a qualitative screen of business activity (§2) and a
quantitative screen of financial structure (§3).

## §2 Sector screening

§2.1 Investment is not permissible in companies whose core business is conventional
interest-based banking or lending, conventional insurance, alcohol production or
distribution, pork products, gambling and casinos, adult entertainment, or weapons
manufacture for illegitimate use.

§2.2 Tobacco and conventional entertainment are treated as prohibited by some
screening bodies and as requiring review by others. Products relying on these
sectors should be referred for review.

§2.3 Where a prohibited activity is incidental rather than core, the quantitative
tolerance at §3.3 applies.

## §3 Financial screening

§3.1 The purpose of financial screening is to exclude companies whose capital
structure depends substantially on interest, even where their business activity is
itself permissible.

§3.2 Illustrative thresholds in common use:

- Interest-bearing debt should not exceed roughly one third of total assets or of
  trailing average market capitalisation.
- Cash and interest-bearing securities should not exceed roughly one third of the
  same measure.
- Income from prohibited sources should not exceed roughly five per cent of total
  revenue.

§3.3 Where incidental prohibited income falls within the tolerance at §3.2, that
portion of any dividend or return must be purified by donation to charity and may
not be retained.

§3.4 Thresholds differ between screening bodies, and the choice of denominator
(total assets or market capitalisation) materially affects the outcome. A product
should state which methodology it applies.

## §4 Ongoing obligations

§4.1 Screening is not a one-time exercise. Holdings must be re-screened at least
each reporting period, since a company's debt ratio or revenue mix may change.

§4.2 A holding that ceases to pass screening must be divested within a reasonable
period, and gains attributable to the non-compliant period purified.

## §5 Assessment criteria

§5.1 A fund or investment product should be judged non-compliant where it has no
screening methodology at all, or where it knowingly includes companies whose core
business falls within §2.1.

§5.2 A product should be referred for review where a screening methodology exists
but its thresholds, denominator, or purification process are unstated.

§5.3 A product should be referred for review where it tracks a conventional index
without a compliant screening overlay.

§5.4 A product may be judged compliant where it applies a stated screening
methodology consistent with §2 and §3, re-screens periodically, and purifies
incidental prohibited income.
