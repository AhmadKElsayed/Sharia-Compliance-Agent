# SFS-002 — Murabaha: Cost-Plus Sale and Ownership Requirements

## §1 Scope

§1.1 This document addresses *Murabaha*, a sale in which the seller discloses the
cost of the asset and adds a stated profit margin, and its use in asset financing.

§1.2 It covers both simple Murabaha and Murabaha to the purchase orderer, in which
a customer requests that the institution acquire an asset and resell it to them.

## §2 Essential conditions

§2.1 The subject of the sale must be a specific, existing, and lawful asset. Money,
debt, and prohibited goods may not be the subject of a Murabaha.

§2.2 The institution must acquire genuine ownership of the asset before selling it
to the customer. Selling an asset one does not own is not permissible.

§2.3 The institution must bear the risks of ownership for the period it holds the
asset, however brief. A transaction structured so that the institution holds legal
title without any exposure to loss, damage, or defect is a financing arrangement in
the form of a sale, and is not permissible.

§2.4 The cost and the profit margin must both be disclosed to the customer.

§2.5 The sale price, once agreed, is fixed for the life of the contract. It may not
be increased on account of late payment or the passage of time.

## §3 Sequence of transactions

§3.1 The customer's promise to purchase, the institution's acquisition of the
asset, and the sale to the customer must occur as distinct steps in that order.

§3.2 Executing the acquisition and the onward sale as a single simultaneous entry,
or backdating documentation to give that appearance, invalidates the transaction.

§3.3 An agency arrangement, in which the customer acts as the institution's agent
to purchase the asset, is permissible but requires care: the agency and the
subsequent sale must be documented as separate contracts, and the institution must
hold real ownership between them.

## §4 Late payment

§4.1 The institution may not charge additional profit for late payment, as this
reproduces riba on a debt. See [SFS-001 §2.1].

§4.2 A late-payment charge is permissible only where it is donated to charity and
does not accrue to the institution's income.

§4.3 The institution may require security, and may accelerate the outstanding debt
on default, provided the amount claimed does not exceed the agreed sale price.

## §5 Assessment criteria

§5.1 A Murabaha-labelled product should be judged non-compliant where the
institution never takes ownership, or takes ownership in form only with no exposure
to ownership risk.

§5.2 A product should be judged non-compliant where the "profit margin" is
recalculated over time or benchmarked so that it accrues with elapsed time rather
than being fixed at contract.

§5.3 Using a conventional interest rate as an external *benchmark* to set a fixed
margin at inception is a matter of ongoing scholarly debate and should be referred
for review rather than decided automatically.

§5.4 A commodity Murabaha used purely to generate cash, with no genuine intent to
trade the underlying commodity, should be referred for review; such structures are
widely criticised as circumventing the prohibition in [SFS-001].
