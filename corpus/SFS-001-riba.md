# SFS-001 — Riba: Prohibition of Interest and Guaranteed Returns

## §1 Scope

§1.1 This document addresses the prohibition of *riba* in deposit-taking, lending,
and investment products offered by an Islamic financial institution.

§1.2 It applies to any arrangement in which one party transfers money to another
with an expectation of return, including savings accounts, term deposits, loans,
and financing facilities.

## §2 Definition

§2.1 *Riba* is any excess or increase received by a lender over and above the
principal amount lent, where that excess is stipulated as a condition of the loan
or accrues purely as a function of time.

§2.2 Two forms are commonly distinguished. *Riba al-nasi'ah* arises from a
time-based increase on a debt. *Riba al-fadl* arises from an unequal exchange of
two quantities of the same fungible commodity in a spot transaction.

§2.3 The prohibition attaches to the *contractual guarantee* of a return, not to
profit as such. Profit earned through genuine trade, leasing, or shared enterprise
risk is permissible.

## §3 Deposit products

§3.1 A deposit account that contractually guarantees the depositor a fixed or
predetermined rate of return constitutes riba and is not permissible. This holds
regardless of whether the return is described as interest, profit, yield, or bonus.

§3.2 A guaranteed return combined with a guarantee of the principal is the defining
characteristic of an impermissible interest-bearing deposit. Where the institution
undertakes both to preserve capital and to pay a predetermined return, the
arrangement is a loan bearing interest in substance, whatever its label.

§3.3 Naming a product an "Islamic savings account" or expressing the return as a
percentage of "profit" does not alter its character. Substance governs form.

## §4 Permissible alternatives

§4.1 A *Mudarabah*-based investment account is permissible, in which the depositor
supplies capital, the institution manages it, and realised profit is shared
according to a ratio agreed in advance. The ratio may be fixed; the *amount* may not.

§4.2 A *Qard Hasan* (benevolent loan) arrangement is permissible for a current
account, where the institution guarantees the principal but pays no return of any
kind. Capital protection without return is acceptable; return without risk is not.

§4.3 *Wadiah* (safekeeping) deposits are permissible where the institution
guarantees the principal and pays no contractual return. A discretionary gift
(*hibah*) may be given, provided it is neither promised, advertised, calculated by
formula, nor made so regular that customers come to expect it as a term.

§4.4 An institution may publish *historical* profit rates for a Mudarabah account.
It may not present an *indicative future* rate in terms that amount to a promise.

## §5 Assessment criteria

§5.1 A product should be judged non-compliant on riba grounds where all of the
following hold: the customer transfers money to the institution; the institution
guarantees repayment of that money in full; and the customer is entitled to a
predetermined additional amount.

§5.2 Where the return varies with the actual performance of an underlying asset or
enterprise, and the customer bears a genuine share of loss, the arrangement is not
riba.

§5.3 Where a product guarantees principal and pays a variable, discretionary,
non-formulaic amount, the arrangement requires case-by-case review rather than
automatic approval, because the discretion may be nominal in practice.
