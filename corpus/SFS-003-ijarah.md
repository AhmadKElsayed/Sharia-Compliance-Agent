# SFS-003 — Ijarah: Leasing and the Risks of Ownership

## §1 Scope

§1.1 This document addresses *Ijarah*, the transfer of the usufruct of an asset for
an agreed rental, and *Ijarah Muntahia Bittamleek*, a lease concluding in transfer
of ownership to the lessee.

## §2 Essential conditions

§2.1 The leased asset must exist, be identified, and be capable of being used
without being consumed. Assets consumed by use, such as money or fuel, cannot be
leased.

§2.2 The use to which the asset is put must be lawful. Leasing premises or
equipment for a prohibited activity is not permissible. See [SFS-006 §2].

§2.3 The lessor must own the asset, or hold a valid right to sub-lease it, for the
whole of the lease term.

§2.4 The rental amount and the lease period must be specified. Rentals may be fixed
for the term or reviewed periodically against a disclosed benchmark, provided each
revised rental is determinable and agreed at the point of revision.

## §3 Allocation of risk and cost

§3.1 The lessor, as owner, bears the risk of total loss or destruction of the asset
not caused by the lessee's negligence or misuse.

§3.2 The lessor bears *major* maintenance — the structural and capital upkeep on
which the asset's continued usability depends. Transferring the entirety of this
obligation to the lessee removes the ownership risk that distinguishes a lease from
a loan, and is not permissible.

§3.3 The lessee bears *operating* maintenance arising from ordinary day-to-day use.

§3.4 Insurance of the asset is the lessor's responsibility as owner. Its cost may be
reflected in the rental. Where takaful cover is available it should be preferred to
conventional insurance. See [SFS-004 §4].

§3.5 Rental ceases to accrue for any period in which the asset is genuinely unusable
through no fault of the lessee. A contract requiring rental to continue throughout a
total loss is not permissible.

## §4 Lease ending in ownership transfer

§4.1 The lease contract and the transfer of ownership must be separate undertakings.
Embedding an automatic, binding sale within the lease contract itself conflates two
contracts in one and is not permissible.

§4.2 Transfer may be effected by gift or by sale at an agreed price at the end of
the term, documented independently of the lease.

§4.3 Until transfer occurs, the lessor remains owner and retains the obligations at
[SFS-003 §3].

## §5 Assessment criteria

§5.1 A lease-labelled product should be judged non-compliant where the lessee bears
all maintenance, all insurance, and the entire risk of loss, since the lessor then
holds title without ownership risk and the arrangement is a secured loan in
substance.

§5.2 A product should be judged non-compliant where rental continues to accrue after
the asset has been destroyed.

§5.3 Where rentals float against a published benchmark, the arrangement should be
referred for review to confirm that each revision is agreed and determinable rather
than accruing automatically with time. Compare [SFS-002 §5.3].
